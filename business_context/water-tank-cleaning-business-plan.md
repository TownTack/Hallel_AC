# Water Tank Cleaning & Disinfection Service
### A subsidiary of Hallel Industries Limited — Business & Financial Plan

*All figures in Ghana cedis (GHS). Equipment, chemical, water and registration prices are indicative market ranges (mid-2026) — confirm with local quotes before you commit. Assumptions are stated so you can adjust them.*

## 1. The concept

Households in your estate — and thousands of similar estates across Ghana — store potable water in tanks for one to two months because mains supply is unreliable. Standing water grows biofilm, sediment and bacteria, yet almost no one cleans their tank. The venture is an **on-request tank cleaning and disinfection service** that restores tanks to a safe, potable standard, with an optional recurring (quarterly) plan.

**Why it works:** a clear, unserved need in your own backyard (200 households as a captive pilot market), low technical complexity, low variable cost per job, and a natural path to recurring revenue and commercial contracts.

**Fit under Hallel Industries Limited:** the service runs as a branded subsidiary/division, drawing on the parent's name, governance, banking and admin structure. That gives instant credibility — important when you are asking people to trust you with their drinking water — and lets you share overheads at the start.

**Competition note:** at least one Ghanaian operator already advertises Polytank cleaning, so this is not a virgin market. That is reassuring (demand is proven) but you must scope their pricing and service quality and decide how you differentiate — branding under Hallel, a no-entry hygienic method, documented water-quality testing, and a written guarantee are your levers.

## 2. The service methodology — a NO-ENTRY SOP

**Design principle: the crew never climbs inside the tank.** Entering a tank is confined-space work, and it carries real hazards — oxygen deficiency, chlorine/gas build-up, slips and falls, drowning in residual water, and re-contaminating a tank you just cleaned with a worker's body and boots. A no-entry method removes all of that. It also avoids confined-space permits, rescue-plan and specialist-training requirements, is faster to set up, and is a genuine selling point: *"no one climbs into your drinking-water tank."*

**No-entry works because** standard household Polytanks (Sumo/Hippo, roughly 1.5–2 m tall with a wide top manhole) can be fully reached from the opening with the right tools. Telescopic brushes reach the walls and floor; an extended pressure lance and a rotating nozzle scour surfaces you can't touch; a long vacuum wand lifts the sludge out.

**The 7-step no-entry protocol** (this is your quality guarantee and training script):

1. **Isolate & drain** — close the inlet; drain via the outlet and a submersible pump; vacuum the last of the water and sediment out **through the manhole**.
2. **Scrub (from outside)** — work telescopic brushes over walls and floor through the opening.
3. **Pressure-wash (from outside)** — strip biofilm, sludge and stains with an extended lance; use a **rotating tank-cleaning (spinner) nozzle** for tanks above ~2,000 L.
4. **Extract** — vacuum/pump out all the dirty water.
5. **Disinfect** — refill to normal level, dose chlorine to a target of **50 mg/L (ppm) free chlorine**, mix well, hold for a **2-hour contact time** (for heavy fouling, shock at 100 ppm for ~1 hr or 200 ppm for ~3 hr). No entry required.
6. **Discharge safely, rinse & neutralise** — drain the chlorinated water to a drain/sewer (**never** into a stream, open gutter, septic system or onto vegetation); rinse and refill with clean water; confirm the free-chlorine residual has fallen to **≤0.5 mg/L** (WHO safe drinking-water residual is 0.2–0.5 mg/L). Add a dechlorination agent or refill again if it is still too high.
7. **Inspect, test, document, hand over** — inspect with a **pole/borescope camera**, test the residual, log it, get the customer's sign-off, and issue a service certificate with the next-due date (this seeds your recurring business).

> **Operational note — water is your key constraint.** Cleaning and rinsing consumes water. In a water-scarce estate, carry rinse water on the truck, schedule around supply days, or offer a lower-priced **"clean-only"** option where the customer refills afterward. Assume the customer's normal supply does the final refill (they were going to fill the tank anyway); you only carry rinse water.
>
> **Large tanks:** no-entry still applies to 10,000 L+ Rambo/industrial and underground tanks, but needs the spinner/jetting nozzle and longer-reach tools — price these as custom commercial jobs.

> **Preserving the client's stored water (holding-tank option).** Because many tanks still hold water when the crew arrives, the standard setup carries a **Rambo-700 (7,000 L) holding tank** on the aboboyaa. Before cleaning, the crew transfers the client's stored water into the holding tank; after the tank is cleaned and disinfected, that water is **filtered** (multi-stage sediment/carbon), transferred back, and dosed to a safe chlorine residual. This "no-waste" promise is a strong differentiator in a water-scarce estate. Three honest caveats: the holding tank and transfer hoses **must be sanitised between every client** to prevent cross-contamination; filtration removes sediment and turbidity but **not pathogens**, so the returned water must still carry a safe residual to be potable; and the extra handling adds time, so consider charging a **premium** for the water-preservation service versus a standard fresh-fill clean.

## 3. Recommended disinfection chemicals (safe and available in Ghana)

| Chemical | Available chlorine | Role | Indicative dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcium hypochlorite (HTH), granular** | 65–70% | **Primary disinfectant** | ~150 g per 2,000 L to reach 50 ppm | Widely sold by pool/water-treatment suppliers. Store dry, away from acids and organics (fire risk). |
| **Sodium hypochlorite (liquid bleach), unscented** | 3.5–12% | Alternative / easy to dose | ~2 L of 5% bleach per 2,000 L to reach 50 ppm | Must be **fragrance- and additive-free**. Degrades over time — buy fresh. |
| **NaDCC tablets (e.g. Aquatabs)** | — | Final maintenance residual, small tanks | Per pack instructions | WHO-recommended for drinking water; stable, easy to carry; Aquatabs is distributed in Ghana. |
| **Sodium thiosulfate** | — | Dechlorination (neutralise excess chlorine) | As needed | Brings residual down fast before handover. |

**Dose formula:** grams of HTH = (target ppm × tank litres) ÷ (1000 × 0.68). Example: 50 ppm in 2,000 L → (50 × 2000) ÷ 680 ≈ **147 g**.

**Never use** scented/thickened/additive household bleach or any cleaner not rated for potable water. **Never mix** chlorine products with each other, with acids, or with ammonia. Because you need only grams per tank, **chemical cost per job is very low** — the economics are driven by labour, water and transport, not chemicals.

## 5. Pricing by Polytank size

Polytank (made in Accra by Poly Products Ltd) is the dominant brand, so pricing to its actual capacities is exactly right. The common household range runs from the small Nano/Sumo units up through the Hippo (1,000–4,000 L) and Sumo (1,000–3,000 L) series to Rambo (large/industrial, up to ~30,000 L). Larger tanks cost more to service — more water to move, more surface to scrub, more time — but each visit carries the same fixed set-up cost, so **bigger tanks are more profitable per job.**

**Two service tiers.** Customers choose between a **Standard clean** (tank cleaned and disinfected; the old stored water is drained and the tank refilled fresh) and a premium **Clean-and-preserve** (the crew transfers the customer's stored water into the Rambo-700 holding tank, filters it, and returns it after cleaning — no water wasted). The premium tier commands a higher price, carries a slightly higher margin, and is what makes the holding-tank kit *earn its keep* rather than just add cost.

## 6. Complementary product sales (an added revenue stream)

Because the crew is already at the tank and has just inspected it, there is a natural, low-effort upsell: supplying and fitting the small water-system parts customers frequently need. This lifts the basket value of each visit at a healthy retail margin — on top of the cleaning fee — and creates repeat sales (replacement filter cartridges) that sit neatly alongside the quarterly plan.

**Items to carry on the aboboyaa:**
- Water filters — inline sediment filters, point-of-entry housings and tap-mounted filters, plus replacement cartridges
- Float / ball valves — the automatic tank shut-off that is very often worn or stuck
- Tank fittings — tank connectors/bulkheads, gate and ball taps, elbows and hose adaptors
- Tank lids/covers and mosquito-proof breather screens — to keep a freshly cleaned tank sealed
- Water-level indicators / gauges
- NaDCC (Aquatabs) sachets — so customers can hold a safe chlorine residual between cleans

**Indicative economics** (confirm against supplier trade prices):
- Retail margin on these parts is typically 30–50%.
- Example: a float valve bought at ~GH₵25–40 and fitted for ~GH₵70–90; an inline filter + housing bought at ~GH₵90–150 and fitted for ~GH₵180–260.
- If about 30% of jobs add one accessory averaging ~GH₵60 gross profit, that is roughly 22 of 72 jobs/month → about **GH₵1,300/month of extra gross profit** for one crew. Modest, but it grows with volume and needs only a small stock float (~GH₵1,500–2,500 of working capital carried on the vehicle).

**Keep it disciplined:** fit only potable-safe, good-quality parts (your reputation depends on it), keep the stock light so capital is not tied up, and treat it as a complement to the core clean — never a distraction from it. As volume grows, negotiate trade pricing with suppliers (Supply Master, Aquasolve, plumbing merchants), and the filter-cartridge replacements become a small recurring annuity.

## 8. Scalability plan

- **Stage 1 (Months 1–3): Prove it.** One crew, your own estate. Nail the SOP, gather before/after photos and testimonials, convert customers to the quarterly plan.
- **Stage 2 (Months 4–9): Multiply.** Add a 2nd and 3rd crew; expand to neighbouring estates. Buy a motor-tricycle/pickup for logistics and rinse-water haulage. Push the **quarterly subscription** — recurring revenue is the real value of the business.
- **Stage 3 (Months 10+): Go commercial.** Target large-tank retainer contracts: schools, clinics/hospitals, hotels, offices, filling stations, food factories, churches. Add a **water-quality testing** service (partner with a lab) and consider cross-selling tanker water delivery.
- **Grow the accessories line.** As call volume rises, deepen the complementary product range (filters, valves, fittings) and lock in trade pricing — cartridge replacements become recurring revenue.
- **Stage 4: Replicate.** Roll out branded branches under Hallel in Tamale, Accra and beyond — a franchise/branch model on one standardised SOP.

**What makes it scale:** a documented no-entry SOP anyone can be trained on, variable (piece-rate) labour, asset-light growth (lease extra kit before buying), and simple phone/WhatsApp booking evolving into a small app.

## 9. Risks & mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Existing competitor(s)** | Scope their pricing/quality; differentiate on Hallel branding, no-entry hygiene, documented testing, and a written guarantee. |
| **Cross-contamination via holding tank** | Sanitise the holding tank and transfer hoses with chlorine between every client; filter and re-dose the returned water to a safe residual. |
| **Water scarcity for rinsing** | Carry rinse water; schedule around supply days; offer a priced "clean-only" option. |
| **Over-chlorination / potability liability** | Strict SOP; test every tank to ≤0.5 mg/L before handover; documented log and sign-off. |
| **Chemical handling accidents** | Full PPE; correct storage (HTH dry, away from acids/organics); never mix chemicals; training. |
| **Working at height (tank tops/roofs)** | Proper ladders, harness for tall tanks, basic public-liability insurance. |
| **Contamination claims** | Keep test records; partner with a water lab; issue certificates; consider insurance. |
| **Low barriers to entry / imitators** | Professionalism, guarantee, staff certification, recurring-plan lock-in. |
| **Seasonality of demand** | Smooth with quarterly subscriptions and commercial contracts. |
| **Cash flow** | Keep labour variable (piece rate); payment on completion or deposit. |

*Note: the no-entry method itself is a major risk reduction — it removes the most dangerous and highest-liability part of tank cleaning.*

## 10. Four-week launch plan

**Week 1 — Set up the business.** Register the subsidiary/division under Hallel (RGD); assign a bank/mobile-money account; finalise brand name, the Polytank-size price list, and the written no-entry SOP; design flyers and signage.

**Week 2 — Procure & equip.** Buy equipment, no-entry tools, PPE and opening chemical stock (get 2–3 quotes each); assemble the kit; print flyers, banner, uniforms and vehicle signage.

**Week 3 — Train & pilot.** Recruit the crew; train on the no-entry SOP, dosing and safety; run **2–3 free/discounted pilot cleans** (start with your own tank and a neighbour's) to refine timing and capture before/after evidence.

**Week 4 — Launch & sell.** Distribute flyers to all 200 households; run a launch offer (15% off first clean, or a free basic water test); take bookings by phone/WhatsApp; serve first paying customers and offer each the quarterly plan on the spot.
